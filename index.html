The main challenge here was the timeline as well as being limited to only 5 people to execute the entire project. We only had a week or two to execute everything from plot writing, storyboarding, recording, and editing.

Once the story and sequence of scenes were finalized, we shot everything in one day and one place. I was the only camera man available and so to pretend that we had multiple cameras, I had to film the same scenes from different angles.

For editing, I had a groupmate with me help me out to stitch half of the scenes together as well as work on the montage scene while I did the color grading as well as credits.

In this project, I was able to utilize time management and extreme collaboration as well as communication with my team.

One of the difficulties I faced with this coursework was that I lacked ideas on what I wanted to express in my craft as the prompt was “borrow from what exists”.

I had to brainstorm as well as organize thoughts and ideas with the help of a mind map that resulted to (hopefully) a meaningful piece.

Additionally, with the video being experimental, I took the chance to apply new video editing techniques that I have always wanted to try out.

I had difficulty mostly with the amount of sources I had to skim through and the assets I had to sort. Editing it was also a whole different kind of challenge as I encountered a heavy lag and it was taking a toll on my hardware at the time.

During this time, I really had to focus and practice organizing my files within the project and timeline to make the workflow easier and faster. To make up for the slow render times, I also had to work with lower resolutions on certain clips to try and make it easier to work on.

Similar with the challenge above, this was mostly a hardware challenge. Motion graphics takes a toll on computers and the amount of lines I had to animate was making everything a bit slow and frustrating to finish on time.

I had to work on lower resolutions and as well as manage my time effectively by sacrificing a bit of rest to push out the introduction video on time for the deadline while still delivering pleasant results.

The locations here are real places and I believe the biggest hurdle was that lack of cooperation from teammates as well as a fast approaching deadline. My skills of being a fast worker, adaptability to the difficult situation and my proficiency in after effects came in real handy.

To address the issue, I had to work extremely fast alongside my friend by shooting photos and filming in one day, and to edit them alone by night and was still submitted on time for the deadline.

This one is a personal project and was the starting point of wherein I took video editing much more seriously with the VFX and motion graphics.

It served as a practice and so many challenges came in such as familiarizing myself with various video editing terms, gathering vfx materials, keyframing, and many more.

I took my time in learning and studying tutorials as well as finding the appropriate websites for assets and other presets that could be used for this and future projects.

This is a coursework for Kinetic Typography. One of the difficulties I had was to make everything more lively and to have more movement. I believe I did not choose the best song either for this project which made things more difficult than it was supposed to be.

To make up for the lack of lyrics and characters that I can play around with, I used more graphics to fill in the gaps and played with visual effects to make it seem more interesting while also connecting the message of the song to the narrative of the animated movie featured in the project.

This is a proof of concept for our proposed capstone project, “Sungka Go!”. One of the difficulties faced in this was making the “gameplay” look realistic and that as if it was a legitimate game.

It took a lot of collaboration on the assets and PNGs while I also had to use my skills in Figma to prototype the whole thing before I was able to import everything into Premiere Pro to create the whole trailer.
